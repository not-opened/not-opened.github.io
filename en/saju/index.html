What is Saju?

In Korean saju (사주), a birth moment becomes four columns of symbols. Here’s how to read the shape of a chart without sharing a single personal detail.

YearMonthDayHour
Four pillarsExplore the chartsThe tradition

Four pillars, eight characters

Each pillar is a pair. A Heavenly Stem goes on top and an Earthly Branch goes below. The pair comes from a repeating calendar cycle: ten stems and twelve branches form sixty combinations in sequence. Four pairs make eight written characters, also called palja (팔자) in Korean.

01 Year
The birth-year pair. Its branch corresponds to a zodiac animal, but that animal is only one of the eight signs.
02 Month
The birth-month pair. It supplies seasonal context in a traditional reading.
03 Day
The birth-day pair. Many readers start with its top character, often called the Day Master.
04 Hour
The time-of-birth pair. Its branch marks a traditional two-hour period; it isn’t the minutes on a modern clock.

Some charts print the columns right to left. This guide starts at the left with the year.

The animal names are labels for the branches, not descriptions of the people who might have them.

Where the chart fits in Korean culture

Saju belongs to a wider East Asian tradition of interpreting the cycles of time. In a historical Korean wedding custom recorded by the National Folk Museum, a prospective groom’s family sent a sajudanja (사주단자): a formal document carrying his birth year, month, day, and hour. The record offered more than a date. It was also part of how the families began the marriage arrangements.

A story, not a verdict. Think of a reading as one way people have told stories about timing and temperament. A chart does not establish personality, predict an outcome, or tell you whom you should marry. You can explore the tradition without asking it to make decisions for you.
