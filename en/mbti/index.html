Korean culture · Conversation guide

Why do Koreans ask for your MBTI?

In some Korean social circles, four letters can be an icebreaker. If someone asks your MBTI, they're often opening a conversation about what you're like. You don't need a type to join in.

MBTI가 뭐예요?

“What's your MBTI type?”

Where you might hear it

At a casual introduction, someone might offer their own type first, then ask yours. A 2022 Korea.net honorary reporter's account of Korean introductions describes this exact question. You can also see it turned into a guessing game in a 2024 clip from Korean broadcaster ENA. Those are examples, not rules for how people across Korea introduce themselves.

It can be a quick way into a conversation about how you spend a weekend or how you like to make plans. Try replying with one concrete preference: “I like to book the train early, but I leave the rest of the day open.” That gives the other person more to work with than a label alone.

What the letters do and don't tell you

The official Myers-Briggs Type Indicator® describes preferences using four pairs: E/I (Extraversion/Introversion), S/N (Sensing/Intuition), T/F (Thinking/Feeling), and J/P (Judging/Perceiving). A preference describes what may feel more natural to someone. It doesn't say what they can do or how they'll behave each time.

In everyday conversation, “MBTI” may also refer to results from a free online quiz. 16Personalities explains that it uses a different framework and adds an -A or -T suffix. Those suffixes aren't part of the official MBTI result. In a cafe chat, it usually matters more what the person means by their letters than which quiz gave them the result.

The Myers & Briggs Foundation's relationship guide does not rank type pairings. If two friends plan differently, ask how much notice each needs rather than guessing whether their types will get along.

What would you say next?

Open the situation that sounds most like yours. These are sample replies, not a test. Nothing you choose is submitted or saved.

I don't know my type

Try: 제 유형은 잘 몰라요. 어떤 유형이세요?

“I'm not sure about my type. What's yours?” If you don't feel like asking back, you can simply say you don't know and change the subject.

I know my type, but it doesn't always fit

Try: 저는 INFP가 나왔는데, 상황에 따라 좀 달라요.

“I got INFP, but it depends on the situation.” Add a real example: you might be quiet in a big group and talkative with close friends.

Someone jokes that our types won't get along

Try: 그것보다, 약속은 미리 잡는 편이에요?

“More importantly, do you usually make plans in advance?” Turn the joke into a question you can both answer from experience.

I'd rather talk about something else

Try: MBTI는 잘 모르는데, 요즘 뭐에 빠져 계세요?

“I don't really follow MBTI. What are you into these days?” A favorite snack, show, or neighborhood is just as good a place to start.
