Dream reflection

Being chased

A pursuer, a blocked path, or the moment you finally reach safety.

What changes when you retell the scene

Picture two versions of a chase. In one, you know exactly who is behind you; in the other, an empty corridor still feels dangerous. Instead of treating both as the same symbol, notice what was clear: the place, the speed, the distance to safety, or the person you wanted nearby. You can leave the pursuer unnamed if you never saw them.

A belief recorded in Korea

An Academy of Korean Studies Korean-culture textbook includes being chased among examples of dreams traditionally read as unfavorable. It presents that reading as a belief, not a scientifically supported rule. A dream about a familiar face is not evidence that the real person intends to hurt you.

Try changing one detail

If retelling feels useful, write one sentence about a different ending. An open shop, a streetlight, or a friend waiting at the exit may be enough. Ask what that change adds to the scene: company, room to move, or a way to stop. No need to replay a frightening dream or search for a person to blame.

One question to keep

Were you trying to get away from someone, reach someone, or simply find a door that opened?
